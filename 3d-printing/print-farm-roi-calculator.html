Methodology

Fleet capacity is estimated from the number of printers, available hours, utilization, print time, and failure rate. Production cost includes material, electricity, depreciation, and hands-on labor, with failure rate applied to the full cost of each print attempt. Selling fees and shipping are modeled separately from production cost. Monthly operating profit subtracts production, selling, shipping, and fixed costs; cash profit adds depreciation back because it is non-cash. Payback and ROI compare cumulative cash profit with the printer fleet's upfront purchase and setup investment.

How to use this for a print-farm scaling decision

Enter the economics of one printer, then set the number of printers and the utilization level you can realistically maintain. Compare modeled production capacity with the number of orders you expect to sell. If sales remain below capacity, adding printers may not change modeled revenue; if capacity is below demand, additional printers can increase the number of successful prints the operation can fulfill.

Limitations of this model

  • The model assumes one successful print per order and does not account for multi-item orders, batching, or shared shipping costs.
  • Utilization is a planning assumption and does not model maintenance downtime, queueing, setup delays, or changing production schedules.
  • Sales volume is treated as a monthly input and does not model seasonality, customer acquisition, or demand growth.
  • Results are estimates for planning purposes only, not financial or investment advice.

Frequently asked questions

How does printer utilization affect a print farm's ROI?

Utilization determines how much of the available printer time is actually used for production. Higher utilization can increase the number of successful prints the fleet can produce, but it only increases revenue when there is enough demand to sell the additional output.

How does this calculator know whether demand or printer capacity is limiting growth?

It compares the monthly sales volume entered with the fleet's modeled successful-print capacity. If sales are below capacity, demand is the limiting factor; if sales meet or exceed capacity, printer capacity is the limiting factor under the assumptions entered.

Why does the failure rate affect more than material cost?

A failed print can consume material, electricity, printer time, and hands-on labor. The calculator therefore applies the failure rate to the full cost of each print attempt rather than treating failure as material waste alone.

Why is depreciation added back when calculating print-farm payback?

Depreciation is an accounting allocation of the printer's purchase cost rather than a monthly cash payment. The calculator includes depreciation in operating cost, then adds it back to estimate monthly cash profit before comparing that cash flow with the fleet's upfront investment.
