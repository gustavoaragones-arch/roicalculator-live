How the 3D Print Price Is Calculated

The calculator estimates a customer price by combining material, electricity, printer depreciation, labor, overhead, and an allowance for failed or reprinted jobs. Platform or payment fees are then included in the quoted price, while the target profit margin is calculated from the final customer price.

  • Material cost is based on grams used and material price per kilogram.
  • Electricity cost uses print time, printer power, and the entered electricity rate.
  • Printer depreciation converts the printer purchase price into an estimated hourly machine cost.
  • Labor includes setup and post-processing time.
  • Failure allowance increases estimated production cost to account for reprints.
  • Platform/payment fees are calculated from the final customer price.
  • Target profit margin is calculated from the final customer price.

How to Use This Calculator

Enter the material cost, print time, electricity cost, printer depreciation, labor, overhead, failure rate, platform fees, and target profit margin for the job. The calculator combines these costs, accounts for expected failed prints, and estimates a price that meets your target margin after platform fees.

  • Use your actual material cost rather than the retail price of a full spool.
  • Include the time you spend preparing, monitoring, finishing, packaging, and handling the order when those activities are part of your labor cost.
  • Increase the failure rate when the job or material has a meaningful risk of failed prints.

The recommended price is a pricing model, not a required market price. Compare it with the customer's requirements, your competitive position, and any additional costs that are specific to the job.

Limitations and Assumptions

The calculator estimates job pricing from the costs and target margin you enter. It does not determine what customers will pay, account for every possible business expense, or predict the actual failure rate of a print.

  • Printer depreciation is modeled from the printer's cost, useful life, and print time entered in the calculator.
  • Failure rate increases the expected cost of a successful print by accounting for failed attempts across the full production cost.
  • Platform fees are applied according to the percentage you enter and may not represent every marketplace or payment-processing charge.
  • Shipping, taxes, refunds, discounts, and other order-specific charges are not automatically included unless you incorporate them into the relevant cost inputs.

Use the result as a pricing and margin-planning estimate. For a customer quote, review the full job scope and any costs that are not represented by the calculator inputs.

Frequently asked questions

What costs should I include when pricing a 3D print service job?

This calculator includes material, electricity, printer depreciation, labor for setup and post-processing, a per-job overhead allowance, and a failure/reprint allowance, then adds platform or payment fees on top before applying your target profit margin.

How does a failed print affect the price I should charge?

The failure/reprint allowance increases estimated production cost before fees and margin are applied, so a higher allowance raises the recommended price to cover the material, time, and labor lost to failed attempts.

How should I account for labor and post-processing time?

Enter your setup and post-processing time and your labor rate; this calculator multiplies the two into a labor cost that is included in production cost before fees and margin, and also used to compute effective hourly earnings.

What is the difference between minimum viable price and recommended price?

Minimum viable price covers production cost and platform/payment fees only, with no profit margin. Recommended price adds your target profit margin on top, so it is higher than the minimum viable price whenever the target margin is greater than zero.
